Week 8 Knowledge Check — AI Spec Development & Capstone

A short retention check covering the week’s core ideas: the AI Spec Development pattern, capstone discovery and the compressed PRD, compressed architecture (TCD/TMD-light), technical-logic validation and finalization, and Friday’s presentations. Answer each question, then check yourself against the key at the end. Aim for concepts, not trivia — every answer maps back to something we used in the capstone.

Format: 12 questions (multiple choice + true/false). ~15 minutes. Individual or triad.

Questions

1. (MC) In the AI Spec Development pattern, the 5-prompt sequence is run how?

    1. All 5 prompts in parallel, then validate the assembled spec once at the end
    1. Each prompt validated before the next runs — a validation chain
    1. As one mega-prompt that includes every input at once
    1. Prompts 1–4 draft the spec; prompt 5 validates all four together

2. (T/F) Senior TPMs use AI Spec Development to draft the spec, not to do the thinking — judgment shapes every section and the AI accelerates the typing.

3. (MC) In the capstone, “compressed” work means:

    1. Lower-quality work produced faster
    1. Selective work — keep what’s load-bearing, cut what’s cosmetic
    1. Only the happy-path sections, skipping edge cases
    1. AI-generated drafts run with lighter validation

4. (MC) In the compressed PRD-light, the acceptance-criteria coverage target is:

    1. 6 happy / 0 sad / 0 weird
    1. 3 happy / 2 sad / 1 weird
    1. 2 happy / 2 sad / 2 weird
    1. 5 happy / 1 sad / 0 weird

5. (MC) When running the AI Spec sequence under time pressure, which content must be preserved verbatim rather than paraphrased by the AI?

    1. The engineering-ready summary paragraphs
    1. Trade-off costs, revisit triggers, named invariants, and customer quotes
    1. The headline and the section titles
    1. The provenance log entries

6. (T/F) In the Day-3 cross-triad spot-check, a reviewer triad that reads the AI Spec and reports “looks fine” without checking claims against the source has done its job correctly.

7. (MC) The 7-point technical-logic validation on Day 4 exists mainly to check:

    1. That each artifact is well-written on its own
    1. Whether the artifacts fit together and cohere as one set
    1. That the AI Spec matches the presentation deck word-for-word
    1. That grammar and formatting are consistent across documents

8. (T/F) Most triads pass all 7 technical-logic checks on the first pass.

9. (MC) In the cross-artifact integration pass, a good cross-reference must be:

    1. Short and informal — “see the TCD” is enough
    1. Specific (cite the §), verified, and add value
    1. Placed only in the AI Spec, not the other documents
    1. Generated by AI to guarantee consistency

10. (MC) On the Friday presentation rubric, which dimension carries the single highest weight?

    1. Customer + problem clarity (15%)
    1. AI Spec quality (20%)
    1. Trade-off honesty (15%)
    1. Presentation craft (15%)

11. (T/F) Triads are scored on a curve — ranked against one another so the scores spread out.

12. (MC) During Q&A, when asked a specific question you cannot answer, the disciplined response is:

    1. Give a confident best guess so you don’t look unprepared
    1. “Honestly, I don’t know — here’s what we’d do to find out”
    1. Immediately defer the question to the instructor
    1. Restate your customer + problem to fill the time

Answer Key

1. B — Each prompt validates before the next runs. The validation chain is what keeps the AI Spec honest; running all five in parallel breaks it.

2. True — “Senior TPMs use this to draft, not to think.” Judgment shapes every section; AI accelerates the typing. AI Spec without validation is fiction.

3. B — Compression is selective, not lower-quality: keep what’s load-bearing, cut what’s cosmetic. Every word earns its place.

4. B — 3 happy / 2 sad / 1 weird. Compressed means fewer ACs, not weaker ones — still Given/When/Then, same 5-failure-mode discipline.

5. B — Trade-off costs, revisit triggers, named invariants, and customer quotes are preserved verbatim; everything else can be synthesized, but hallucinated claims get cut.

6. False — Watch for rubber-stamping. The reviewer should find at least one weak claim; “looks fine” without checking against the source is the failure mode.

7. B — Each artifact may be fine on its own; the 7 checks ask whether they fit together (e.g., SLO budget fits the sequence diagram, data model supports the API).

8. False — Most triads fail 2–3 checks on the first pass. That’s expected — that’s why the finalization day exists.

9. B — Specific (cite the §), verified, and adds value. “See the TCD” without a section number is a sign the writer never actually verified it.

10. B — AI Spec quality at 20% is the highest-weighted dimension — the week’s signature artifact carries the most weight.

11. False — Score against the rubric, not against other triads. Whole-number scores, no curve: if two triads earn 3.5, both are 3.5.

12. B — “Honestly, I don’t know — here’s what we’d do to find out.” Curiosity, not defensiveness; a well-handled “I don’t know” beats a hand-wave.
