Block 1 — What a North Star Actually Is

And the three ways the term gets misused

📚 The Working Definition

A North Star is the single metric whose movement signals that the customer is winning AND the business is winning, simultaneously.

It has three required parts:

PartTest
Customer outcomeA verb the customer would recognize, not a feature
User segmentSpecific enough to exclude users you don't serve
Single numberOne chart, one direction, one team accountable
If the NS lacks any of three, the team will fight about which version they meant. Force the discipline.

🚨 The Three Standard Pitfalls

PitfallLooks likeThe tell
Vanity dressed up"Total active users", "ARR"Customers don't feel it directly when it moves
Leading-indicator confusion"Time-to-first-value"Can move without long-term customer benefit
Gameable target"DAU" with no quality gateAchievable via tactics that don't earn it
The trap: A real NS smells like a KPI to the inexperienced PM. A vanity metric also smells like a KPI. The difference is whether you can name the customer outcome behind it.
Use FieldPulse: "ARR" is fine as a financial KPI; it is *not* an NS because dispatchers don't feel ARR moving.

🌐 Public-Company Examples

👥 Activity 1 — Three Pitfalls Triage

Triads • 35 minutes

Your triad receives 8 NS candidates from public companies (anonymized). For each: assign 0–3 pitfalls present, with a one-line argument.

  • Find the one card that gets a clean bill of health
  • Find the card that gets all three pitfalls
  • Be ready to defend both choices
⏱ 15 min triage · 10 min argue · 10 min readout prep
"Songs Saved" and "Successful Deliveries per Active Customer" are the trickiest. Both reward close reading.

Block 2 — Drafting Your NS

From placeholder to defensible

✏️ The NS Template

Three blanks, no exceptions:

We are winning when [customer outcome]
increases for [user segment],
measured by [single number].

FieldPulse worked example:

"We are winning when completed reconcile-flow runs per dispatcher per shift increases for HVAC dispatchers in shops with 50–200 technicians, measured by weekly completed-flow rate."
Read it slowly. Each blank failing is a different bug. Triads should call out each blank as they fill it.

🛡️ The Counter-Metric (the new tool)

A counter-metric is a second metric that, if it moves the wrong way, signals you're winning the NS for the wrong reasons. It is the guardrail.

NSCounter-metricWhat it catches
Hours of returned dispatcher time% of dispatchers calling support after the new flowAuto-flow that moved time but broke trust
Tickets resolved without escalationRepeat-ticket rate from same customerClosing tickets without solving them
Active accounts in week 130-day retention of those accountsActivation that doesn't stick
The counter-metric is the single most useful new concept today. It survives every challenge season after season.

👥 Activity 2 — Re-write Your NS

Triads • 40 minutes

Each triad member proposes one NS candidate. Pitfall-test all three. Converge on one. Add a counter-metric.

  • 15 min: each member drafts a candidate
  • 15 min: pitfall-test all three (vanity, leading-indicator, gameable)
  • 10 min: write the NS Defense Card with rebuttal answers + counter-metric
⏱ 15 min draft · 15 min test · 10 min defense card
Push back on triads who agree too quickly. Disagreement here is healthy and produces sharper NSs.

Block 3 — The "CEO and Dispatcher" Test

One NS, two audiences, no translation

👨‍💼 👨‍🔧 Two Audiences, Same Number

A defensible NS lands with the CEO (resource allocator) and with the target customer (the person whose life it represents) without translation.

The CEO probes

  • "What if it goes up 20%? What does that earn the company?"
  • "Why this metric, not [adjacent KPI]?"
  • "How do we know we won, not lost?"

The customer probes

  • "What changes about my Wednesday afternoon?"
  • "What do I feel when it goes up?"
  • "Why would I tell a colleague this got better?"
If the NS only lands with one audience, it isn't an NS — it's a KPI in disguise.

🔗 Walk Down, Walk Up

Walk down

From the NS to your operational signals: do they each plausibly contribute to it? Drop or replace any that don't.

Walk up

From the NS to a business outcome leadership cares about: revenue, retention, time-to-value. Can you trace it?

If both fail

Your NS is a KPI in disguise. Re-pick the customer outcome layer.

The Tier Sheet, refreshed: NS at the top, KPIs and operational signals re-checked against it. Coherence above all.
The walk-down/walk-up is fast — 5 minutes. Triads who skip it ship incoherent Tier Sheets.

👥 Activity 3 — "CEO and Dispatcher"

Triad pairs • 40 minutes

Pair with another triad. Take turns being defender and challenger. Challenger plays CEO for 4 min, then dispatcher for 4 min.

  • Use the persona scripts in your packet (or paraphrase)
  • Defender records the strongest challenge from each role
  • Note: this is rehearsal, not stand-up comedy — commit to the role
⏱ 16 min round 1 · 16 min round 2 · 8 min capture
The CEO-then-dispatcher cadence forces triads to hold both audiences at once. Don't let them skip a role.

Block 4 — Lock In

Replace the placeholder, refresh the Tier Sheet, present

✅ The Lock-in Checklist

  • NS uses the template (outcome / segment / number)
  • All three pitfall rebuttals are written, not waved away
  • Counter-metric is named and concrete
  • CEO challenge is captured and addressed
  • Dispatcher challenge is captured and addressed
  • Walk-down: every operational signal still belongs
  • Walk-up: it rolls up to a business outcome leadership cares about
If a triad is missing more than two boxes, they're not done. Push back.

👥 Activity 4 — Lock-In + Refresh

Triads • 45 minutes • Readouts

Final NS lock-in, walk-down/walk-up of the Tier Sheet, and a 90-second readout per triad.

  • 10 min: lock the NS
  • 20 min: walk-down (signals) and walk-up (KPIs & business outcome)
  • 15 min: polish Defense Card and Tier Sheet for sharing
⏱ 10 lock · 20 walk · 15 polish · 90s readouts
Pin both artifacts on the wall when triads finish. They'll be referenced every day for the rest of the week.
