Block 1 — Three Tiers, Three Audiences

Why we say "metric" too loosely

🏠 The Metrics Pyramid

North Star — "Are we winning?"

KPIs — "Is the business healthy?"

Operational signals — "What do we do today?"
The shape matters: few NSs (1), few KPIs (2–4), many operational signals (4–10). A team that has 10 KPIs has 0 KPIs.
When someone says "metric," ask "to whom?" The CEO does not want operational signals; the squad cannot act on a North Star.

📚 Definitions That Hold Up Under Pressure

TierDefinitionRhythmAudience
North StarThe single number that says "the customer is winning AND the business is winning"Quarterly to annualWhole company
KPIBusiness outcome that the NS rolls up to or directly signalsWeekly to monthlyLeadership / board
Operational signalDaily/hourly leading indicator a squad can act onReal-time to dailySquad / TPM
The TPM job: own the operational signals, defend the KPIs, contribute to the North Star.
Most TPMs spend 80% of their measurement attention at the operational-signal layer. That is correct.

🤯 The Vanity Metric Trap

A vanity metric is one that goes up when something happens that doesn't actually matter, and can't be moved by a deliberate decision.

❌ Vanity

  • Total signups (no churn signal)
  • Page views
  • Features shipped
  • Average session length

✅ Actionable

  • Activated accounts in week 1
  • Time-to-first-value
  • % of intended workflows completed
  • Repeat-use within 7 days
The session-length question is the most useful for FieldPulse: a dispatcher who *hates* the app would have low session lengths because they finished fast. Length is not a value signal.

Block 2 — Operational Signals

The metrics a squad watches in standup

🔎 A Good Operational Signal

  • Latency: moves within a day or less
  • Specificity: changes when one thing in the product changes
  • Actionability: if it drops 30%, the team has a meeting tomorrow
  • Causal: plausibly rolls up to a KPI
  • Measurable today — or honestly named as an instrumentation gap
Coach's note: "We don't measure that today" is not a failure — it's a finding. Surface it.
Often, half of a good signal list is "we don't have this data yet." That feeds Weeks 4–5.

🛡️ FieldPulse Worked Example

Problem statement (Week 1): "Dispatchers spend 45 minutes after-shift reconciling tickets, truck stock, and timecards."

Operational signalSourceAction if it dips
Median reconcile-flow durationApp instrumentationInspect last release for regressions
% of dispatchers using mobile reconcile vs paperBackend eventsRe-investigate adoption blockers
Reconcile-flow abandonment rateFunnel analyticsUX review of last drop-off step
Manual override entries per dispatcher per dayInstrumentation gapIndicates trust loss in automation
Notice the override-entries gap. That's a signal we *should* have but don't. Surface gaps openly — they become Week 5 conversations.

👥 Activity 2 — Operational Signal Sprint

Triads • 40 minutes

For your triad's Week-1 problem statement: brainstorm 12 candidate signals (4 per person), then cull to 6 finalists.

  • For each: what it measures, where the data comes from, how fast it moves, what action you'd take
  • Mark at least 2 in red as instrumentation gaps
  • Add the 6 to your triad's Metrics Tier Sheet (bottom row)
⏱ 10 min solo · 15 min cull · 15 min annotate
Walk the room. The most common failure is signals that are leading and unmeasurable today, both equally hand-waved.

Block 3 — KPIs

Where the squad's work shows up in business outcomes

🎯 The Four-Question KPI Quality Bar

A well-formed KPI passes all four:

  1. Spoken in business terms (revenue, retention, time-to-value, NPS) — not feature terms
  2. A senior leader cares about it without translation
  3. You can imagine a single chart that shows it weekly
  4. You can name what's "good" without inventing a number — direction is fine, the number is set later
If a KPI fails any of the four, it's either an op-signal in disguise or a vanity metric.

🧮 Laddering: Signals Up to KPIs

Step

  • Take each operational signal
  • Ask: "If this moves 30% for a quarter, which business outcome shifts?"
  • Cluster signals that ladder to the same outcome
  • Each cluster → candidate KPI

Worked: FieldPulse

  • Reconcile-duration + abandonment + manual-overrides → "Dispatcher hours returned per week"
  • Mobile-reconcile adoption → "% of accounts on the new workflow"
  • Both ladder to NRR retention long-term
Often two operational signals ladder to one KPI. That's a strong signal cluster. One signal that ladders to nothing should make you suspicious.

🔗 The Believable Causal Chain

Your Tier Sheet is only as strong as its narrative: operational signal → KPI → North Star. If you can't tell that story in three sentences, the chain is broken.

FieldPulse three-sentence chain:
"Median reconcile-flow duration drops → dispatchers spend less time on after-shift admin (operational). Hours-returned-per-week climbs → account-level retention conversations get easier (KPI). Net retention rate climbs → FieldPulse is winning (North Star)."
The three-sentence chain is the most useful single artifact you'll teach this week. Have triads rehearse theirs out loud.

👥 Activity 3 — KPI Laddering

Triads • 40 minutes

Cluster your 6 operational signals into 2–3 KPI clusters. Stress-test each KPI against the four-question quality bar.

  • Write the three-sentence causal chain for each cluster
  • Name one signal that doesn't ladder to anything — and decide whether to keep or drop it
  • Add KPIs to your Metrics Tier Sheet (middle row)
⏱ 15 min cluster · 15 min stress-test · 10 min chain narration
Some signals will resist clustering. That's fine — flag them. Sometimes those are the most valuable signals; sometimes they're noise.

Block 4 — Tier Sheet Defense

Before the squad ships, the metrics get challenged

⚔️ Three Standard Challenges

ChallengeQuestionWhat it surfaces
Causal chain"Show me how a change in op-signal #3 plausibly moves your KPI"Hand-waved laddering
Gameability"Pick the easiest of these to game. How would a sales-incentivized team game it?"Metrics that reward bad behavior
Instrumentation"Which two of these can you actually measure today, in production?"Strategy that depends on data we don't have
Watch for: "We'll instrument it" — a real answer also says by when and at what cost.
These three challenges show up again in Week 5 (technical infrastructure) and Week 6 (stakeholder negotiation). They are durable.

⭐ The North Star Placeholder

You're not going to nail your North Star today. Tomorrow is its full treatment. Today: write a placeholder.

We are winning when [customer outcome] increases for
[user segment], measured by [single number].

FieldPulse example placeholder:

"We are winning when after-shift reconcile time drops for HVAC dispatchers in mid-sized shops, measured by median minutes from clock-out to ticket-closeout."
A placeholder is a target to react to. Tomorrow we'll prove or disprove it.

👥 Activity 4 — Tier Sheet Defense

Triads • 45 minutes • Readouts

Pair-defense round: each triad pairs with another. Defender presents the Tier Sheet (4 min). Challenger uses the Three Standard Challenges.

  • 15 min: draft your North Star placeholder using the template
  • 20 min: pair-defense (4 min present, 4 min challenge per direction)
  • 10 min: capture the strongest challenge as a "known weakness" line
⏱ 15 min NS · 20 min defense · 10 min refine
Collect Tier Sheets at end of day. They become wallpaper for the rest of the week.
