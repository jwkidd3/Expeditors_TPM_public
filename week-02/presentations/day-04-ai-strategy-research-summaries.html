Block 1 — Strategy-Scale Summarization

Beyond a single document, beyond a single prompt

📚 What's Different at Strategy Scale

Week 1 promptingStrategy-scale prompting
Single document or short brief5–15 documents, often contradictory
One prompt, one outputIterated prompts, validated output
"Summarize this""Synthesize across these, tag evidence"
You verify confidently in 5 minutesYou only catch fabrication if you're looking
Mindset shift: at strategy scale, you are not consuming the AI's output — you are auditing it. The unit of work is the prompt + the validation pass.
Most participants will under-validate by default. The Provenance Log is the forcing function.

⚠️ Four Failure Modes (the ones that bite)

FailureWhat it looks likeFix
Theme inflation3 distinct complaints lumped under "usability"Demand verbs, not categories; cap themes at 5–7
Citation invention"Ticket #4" doesn't existVerify every cited source exists; demote uncited claims
Premature confidencePlausible synthesis with no acknowledgment of gapsForce a "what's missing" prompt as second turn
Source biasThe AI weights the loudest source highest, regardless of accuracyAsk: "How would your synthesis change if I removed Source X?"
All four failure modes appeared in the prior cohort's first attempt. Surface them as inevitable, not as judgments of the AI.

📝 The Validated Synthesis Prompt

Role: Research analyst supporting a TPM.
Context: Below are [N] interview transcripts and [M] support tickets
from [user segment]. I am investigating [problem statement].
Task: Extract the top 5 recurring pain themes.
Constraints:
  - Each theme must cite 2+ specific source identifiers (e.g., "Interview 1, Ticket #4")
  - Themes must be verbs, not categories
  - Flag any theme you inferred without direct evidence
  - At the bottom, list 3 questions the sources do NOT answer
Format: Markdown list; each entry: Theme / Evidence / Confidence (H/M/L) /
        Inferred? (Y/N). End with "What's missing" section.
This is the single most-reused prompt of Week 2. Triads should add it to their Pattern Library after today.

Block 2 — The FieldPulse Research Pack

Real materials, real noise, real strategy implications

📁 What's in the Pack

  • 3 anonymized dispatcher interview transcripts (~5 pages each)
  • 14 support tickets (already familiar from Week 1 Day 4)
  • 1 industry analyst report (5 pages)
  • 2 competitor product tour transcripts
  • 1 internal "voice of customer" Slack export (50 messages)
Be honest: the pack is noisy, partly contradictory, and has gaps. That's the point. Real strategy work feels like this.
The Slack export is the most tempting source — it's high-volume and easy to summarize. It's also the most biased source. Coach for that bias.

🧑‍🔬 The Cross-Validation Step

Each triad member takes a different theme produced by the AI and verifies every citation against the source. Mark any that fail.

  • If a citation can't be verified → cut the citation, lower theme confidence to L
  • If 2+ citations cut from one theme → cut the theme or relabel as "hypothesis to validate"
  • If a quote is paraphrased misleadingly → flag in provenance log
Don't trust: the AI's confidence rating about its own output. Independent verification is the only signal.
This is the moment most TPMs skip in real work. Today is the rehearsal of the discipline.

👥 Activity 2 — The Research Pack Pass

Triads • 40 minutes

Use your iterated prompt on the real FieldPulse Research Pack. Cross-validate every citation.

  • 5 min pre-flight (confirm prompt has constraints)
  • 15 min run (split pack across 2–3 calls if needed)
  • 15 min cross-validate (one theme per member)
  • 5 min edit (cut failed themes; lower confidence)
⏱ 5 prep · 15 run · 15 verify · 5 edit
Surface in the room: "How many themes did your triad cut?" Normalize cutting. Triads who cut nothing didn't actually verify.

Block 3 — The Competitive Snapshot

The brightest line in AI-assisted research

🛇 The Bright Line

AI can summarize and structure source material you provide.
AI cannot tell you what your competitors actually do.

✅ Safe (you provide source)

  • "Structure these two product tour transcripts into a comparison matrix"
  • "Identify the 3 most important questions these materials don't answer"
  • "Tell me what's missing relative to my own product"

❌ Unsafe (AI invents)

  • "What is ServiceTitan's market share?"
  • "Who is the leader in dispatch SaaS?"
  • "Cite a study showing dispatcher productivity gains"
The "unsafe" prompts will all return confident, plausible, fabricated answers. They are the single highest-cost mistake a TPM makes with AI.

🔗 The Two-Prompt Pattern

Prompt 1 — Structure what we know:

Role: Competitive analyst.
Context: Pasting in 2 competitor product tour transcripts plus
their public pricing pages.
Task: Build a comparison matrix.
Constraints:
  - Use only facts from the materials I pasted
  - Do NOT pull from your training data
  - Flag any cell where the source is silent
Format: Markdown table, with citation column.

Prompt 2 — Tell us what's missing:

From the matrix above, what 3 questions about these competitors
do the source materials NOT answer? For each, suggest where a
TPM should look (analyst reports, customer interviews, public
earnings calls).
Prompt 2 is the highest-leverage prompt of the day. It converts AI from a "knowledge generator" to a "research planner."

👥 Activity 3 — Competitive Snapshot

Triads • 40 minutes

Use the two-prompt pattern on the competitor materials in the Research Pack. Build a matrix and a list of unanswered questions.

  • 15 min: structure-what-we-know prompt + verify cells
  • 15 min: what's-missing prompt + decide which gaps matter
  • 10 min: write the Competitive Snapshot section of your brief
⏱ 15 structure · 15 missing · 10 write
Walk the room. If any triad asks AI "what is the market size" — pause and use it as a teaching moment for the whole cohort.

Block 4 — Brief Assembly + Provenance

Stitch into a brief, write the parts AI cannot, log the parts AI did

📋 The Strategy Brief Structure

  1. Executive summary — hand-written, 5 bullets max
  2. Top 5 user pain themes — AI-summarized, evidence-tagged, cross-validated
  3. Competitive snapshot — AI-drafted from your materials, hand-edited
  4. Strategy implications — hand-written; ties themes to NS and design principles
  5. Provenance log — mandatory; documents every AI use and validation step
Hand-written sections matter most: they are where the TPM's judgment shows. AI does the volume; you do the meaning.
If a triad submits an all-AI brief, push back hard. The hand-written sections are non-negotiable.

📋 The Provenance Log

A trust-building artifact: documents where AI was used, which prompts, what validation was done, and where the AI was wrong.

## Provenance log

### AI-generated sections
| Section | Prompts (link) | Validation | Findings |
|---------|----------------|------------|----------|
| Pain themes | Pattern Library #3 | All citations verified | Cut 1 hallucinated theme |
| Competitive matrix | Pattern Library #5 | Manual check vs source | Flagged 2 silent cells |
| Missing questions | Pattern Library #6 | Triad consensus | Adopted 2 of 3 |

### Where AI was wrong
- Lumped "reconcile" and "timecard" into single theme; we split them
- Inferred a "training" pain theme with no source; cut entirely
- Cited "Ticket #16" — does not exist; replaced with #11

### Manual hand-written sections
- Executive summary
- Strategy implications
- This log
A brief without a Provenance Log is fiction. The log is what allows the brief to be reviewed by a skeptical engineer or director.
