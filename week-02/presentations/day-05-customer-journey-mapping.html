Block 1 — What a Journey Map Is

Anchor in evidence before debating fixes

✅❌ Is / Is Not

IsIs not
Timeline of user actions, thoughts, emotionsFlowchart of your product's screens
Grounded in observed + reported behaviorHow you wish people used your product
Specific to one persona, one job"General user" map
A tool to find friction and leverageAn end-state living in a wiki
The TPM job: not the prettiest map. Find where friction is highest, then connect those points to features that move metrics.
The most common rookie mistake is mapping the product, not the user. Every triad will be tempted. Coach against it.

📋 The Five Swim Lanes

1. Stage — Phases of the journey (5–8 stages)
2. Actions — Concrete verbs the user is doing
3. Thoughts — Quotes from interviews where possible
4. Emotions — 4-point curve, plotted
5. Touchpoints — Every system & person; star the friction
Watch: the emotions curve must not be flat. A flat curve means the triad is sanitizing.
Use stickies if possible — physical mapping forces decisions and surfaces disagreements.

🎯 Pick One Persona, Pick One Scope

Pre-shift → end-of-shift

A single workday. Best for journeys where time-of-day matters.

First-30-days

Onboarding-heavy. Best for surfacing time-to-first-value friction.

Mid-shift exception

"Tech calls out sick." The canonical disruption. High-fidelity friction.

One persona, one scope. Not "all users, all situations." That's the difference between strategy and a wishlist.
If a triad picks "all journeys" — push back hard. Specificity is where insight lives.

👥 Activity 1 — Anchor the Journey

Triads • 35 minutes

Pick one persona, one scope. Stage the journey (5–8 stages). Sanity-check against Week-1 evidence.

  • 5 min: confirm persona
  • 10 min: pick scope
  • 15 min: stage the journey on the canvas
  • 5 min: tag any inferred stage
⏱ 5 persona · 10 scope · 15 stage · 5 evidence-check
The most common stumbling block: stages that are feature names, not user-task names. "Login" is not a stage; "Arriving at the office" is.

Block 2 — Fill the Lanes

Thoughts, emotions, touchpoints, friction stars

🌐 Non-Product Touchpoints (the part most maps miss)

For every stage, list every system or person the user interacts with — including paper, phone, other software, manager, customer.

❌ Incomplete map

  • Stage: "Reconcile"
  • Touchpoint: "FieldPulse mobile"

Misses the actual journey.

✅ Honest map

  • Stage: "Reconcile"
  • Touchpoints: FieldPulse mobile, paper ticket book, tech's phone, owner's spreadsheet, the printer that jammed

Surfaces the integration story.

Bridge to Week 4: non-product touchpoints become the input to system-component mapping in Week 4.
Most triads will want to map only their own product. Force the question: "What else is in their hand?"

⭐ The Friction Star

Place a star on every stage where the user is fighting the journey. Friction stars should cluster. If everything is friction, nothing is.

  • 2–3 stages with stars → healthy map
  • 5+ stages with stars → you're not discriminating; re-rank by severity
  • 0 stages with stars → you sanitized the journey; re-listen to interviews
The clustering check is simple but high-yield. Walk the room and call it out where missing.

👥 Activity 2 — Fill the Lanes

Triads • 40 minutes

Populate Thoughts, Emotions, Touchpoints. Star the friction. Walk-through review by one member.

  • 10 min: thoughts (use real interview quotes when possible)
  • 10 min: emotions curve (must vary)
  • 15 min: touchpoints (include non-product)
  • 5 min: walk-through narration
⏱ 10 thoughts · 10 emo · 15 touch · 5 walk
Hand each triad a marker for friction stars. Physicality of starring forces commitment.

Block 3 — From Friction to Features

Three feature concepts that pass the strategy test

✅ The Three-Test Bar

A feature concept earns a place if it passes all three:

TestQuestion
Friction linkWhich specific stage and friction does it reduce?
Metric linkWhich operational signal or KPI from your Tier Sheet does it move?
Principle linkWhich of your 3 design principles does it align with (or which does it deliberately trade off against)?
The recurring failure: features without metric links. Force the link or kill the feature.
A feature with no metric link cannot be defended in Week 6 stakeholder negotiation. Plant the discipline now.

📄 The Feature Card Template

## Feature concept: 

**Stage(s) addressed:** 
**Friction reduced:** 
**Metric it moves:** 
**Aligned principle:** 
**Trade-off / risk:** 
**Validation:** how would we know it actually moved the metric in 30 days?
Trade-off / risk is non-negotiable. "No downside" is a failed answer. Every choice forecloses another.
The validation row is the bridge to Week 7 (Agile delivery). Plant it now.

👥 Activity 3 — From Friction to Features

Triads • 40 minutes

Brainstorm 8 candidates. Apply the three-test bar. Cull to 3 with full feature cards.

  • 15 min: brainstorm (volume first)
  • 15 min: three-test scoring
  • 10 min: cull to 3 + complete cards
⏱ 15 brainstorm · 15 score · 10 cull
If a triad's 3 features all hit the same stage, ask whether that stage really is 80% of the friction. Force a defense.

Block 4 — Friday Readouts

The full Week-2 strategy package, 12 minutes

🎤 The 12-Minute Package

TimeSectionWhat you cover
1 minNS + counter-metricStatement, segment, number
2 minTier Sheet causal chainThree sentences, op-signal → KPI → NS
2 minOne design principle in detailThe one with most expected pushback
4 minJourney map walk-throughPersona, scope, friction stars

All three voices speak. No single triad-spokesperson. Engineering judges lecturers harshly.
Time is enforced. The clock matters. Triads who blow their time get docked on coherence.

👥 Activity 4 — Readouts

Triads • 12 min present + 3 min Q&A each

Assemble the package. Rehearse with the clock. All three voices on stage.

  • 15 min: assemble & order artifacts
  • 15 min: rehearse with timer

If a triad is way under-rehearsed, give them an extra 10 minutes by trimming the predict step. Rehearsal is the most-skipped step and the most predictive of readout quality.
