Block 1 — The Shared Vocabulary

Nielsen's 10, plus three TPM lenses

🗣️ Why TPMs Need This Vocabulary

Designers and engineers will both override your reaction with their craft language. The TPM job is to name what's wrong in the language they will accept, then defend why it matters with metrics.

❌ Without vocabulary

"This screen feels weird. Can we redo it?"

Loses the conversation in 30 seconds.

✅ With vocabulary

"This screen violates Nielsen #5 (error prevention) and #6 (recognition over recall). The cost shows up in our reconcile-abandonment signal."

Wins the conversation.

This isn't about being right. It's about being able to argue in the trade-off room.

📚 Nielsen's 10 Heuristics

#HeuristicOne-line
1Visibility of system statusThe user always knows what just happened and what's happening now
2Match real worldUse the user's words, not the system's
3User control & freedomUndo, exit, escape — always
4Consistency & standardsSame word, same color, same place
5Error preventionMake destructive actions hard, not easy
6Recognition over recallShow options, don't make me remember them
7Flexibility & efficiencyBeginners get hand-holding, experts get shortcuts
8Aesthetic & minimalistEvery screen element earns its place
9Recover from errorsThe error message names the field and the fix
10Help & documentationFindable, in-context, searchable
Don't memorize the list. Memorize the *frequency of violation* — #1, #5, and #9 are the recurring offenders in B2B SaaS.

🔍 Three TPM-Specific Lenses

11. Time-to-first-value

Can a new user produce a useful result in their first session? Measured in minutes, not features.

12. Failure-mode dignity

How does the product behave when the network drops, the data is wrong, or the user is rushed? Failure is part of UX.

13. Power-user respect

Does the product reward repeat use with shortcuts, or punish it with the same friction every time?

Why these three: they're the heuristics designers most often skip in early-stage products and most often blame on engineering later.
The "failure-mode dignity" lens is especially load-bearing for a logistics/dispatch tool — the product runs in trucks with bad signal.

Triads • 35 minutes

Walk through a B2B SaaS none of your triad uses (provided link). Each member silently captures 3 heuristic violations.

Block 2 — The A11y Floor

The 8 checks every TPM should run before any PRD ships

♿️ Why a Floor, Not a Ceiling

Accessibility is a regulatory floor (ADA, Section 508, EAA), an SEO floor, and a usability ceiling. Fixes for keyboard-only users almost always fix mouse-user errors too.

The TPM responsibility: not to be the a11y expert, but to refuse to ship a feature that fails the floor — and to spot failures unsolicited.
Most squads have a designer who knows a11y deeply. They will love a TPM who flags floor violations early. Use this to win allyship.

✅ The 8-Check Floor

CheckToolFail looks like
Keyboard-focusable interactivesTab through the pageFocus disappears
Visible focus indicatorTab through the pageNo outline on focus
Color contrast 4.5:1WebAIM contrast checkerLight gray on white
Form fields have labelsScreen reader / inspect"Edit text, blank"
Images have alt textView sourceEmpty alt on meaningful image

Touch targets ≥ 44pxMobile devtoolsButtons too small
Each check is < 90 seconds. The whole floor is < 15 minutes. There is no excuse for skipping it.

👥 Activity 2 — A11y Floor Audit

Triads • 40 minutes

Run the 8-check floor on the FieldPulse mobile or your chosen surface. Split the checks across triad members so all 8 are covered (plus one overlap).

  • Each fail: which UX heuristic is also being violated?
  • Output: one-page A11y Floor Audit for the surface
⏱ 25 min audit · 10 min cross-link to heuristics · 5 min summarize
If a triad finishes early, push them to score severity. Most triads under-rate failures because they're not living the keyboard-only experience.

Block 3 — Audit Your Surface

Apply the calibrated eye + the floor to the triad's chosen flow

📋 The Audit Template

  • Top 3 violations with heuristic + observation + cost + severity (1–5)
  • Top 3 strengths — what does this product do better than the average B2B SaaS?
  • A11y Floor Audit result (Pass / Partial / Fail)
  • TPM Lens findings: time-to-first-value, failure-mode dignity, power-user respect
  • One question you cannot answer without the design team
Strengths matter: a "kill list" of violations alienates designers. Naming what works builds trust to address what doesn't.
Most early TPMs over-index on violations. Force the strengths column.

📊 Severity Scoring (1–5)

ScoreMeaningExample
5User task failure or data loss"Submit" button unlabeled — user can't complete reconcile
4Recurring frustration; users work aroundDate picker requires 5 taps — users keep paper logs
3Slows users; stopwatch would noticeTab order skips "amount" — users mouse over
2Cosmetic; affects trustInconsistent button styling
1Edge caseTooltip cut off in landscape
Severity is the single most-disputed score. Encourage debate — that's the design conversation triads should be rehearsing.

👥 Activity 3 — Heuristic Audit of Your Surface

Triads • 40 minutes

Use the audit template on your triad's chosen flow. Severity-score each violation. Capture strengths.

  • 10 min: solo walk-through (each member captures 3+ findings)
  • 15 min: pool + score severity
  • 10 min: write up the template (top 3 violations + strengths)
  • 5 min: name the one question only design can answer
⏱ 10 solo · 15 pool · 10 write · 5 question
The "one question for design" is high-leverage — it shows the participant what their design partner uniquely brings.

Block 4 — From Audit to Principles

The keeper artifact: 3 design principles your squad can hold the line on

🎯 What a Design Principle Looks Like

A design principle is a statement, not a feature. It is decision-making — when two options compete, the principle picks one.

✅ GoodWhy
"If a dispatcher can't recover from a mis-tap in 1 second, we built it wrong."Specific, decision-making, memorable
"Show network state on every screen — assume connectivity is broken."Names a constraint, drives concrete choices
"Power users get keyboard shortcuts. New users get them visible too."Resolves a tension explicitly
❌ BadWhy
"Be user-friendly"Vacuous
"Use Material Design"A toolkit, not a principle
"Be consistent"True but not actionable
The test: would an engineer quote the principle back at you in standup? If yes, it's a principle. If no, it's a slogan.

✂️ Cull to Three

  1. Cluster audit findings into themes. Each theme suggests a principle.
  2. Draft 5 candidate principles. Some will be bad. That's fine.
  3. Test each: when two options compete, does this tell us what to choose?
  4. Cull to 3. Three is the limit for memorability.
  5. Write each principle as one line + one example of a decision it would force.
Three is a hard ceiling. Triads who want four are not yet committed enough to any of them.

👥 Activity 4 — Three Design Principles

Triads • 45 minutes • Readouts

Convert your audit into 3 design principles your squad can hold the line on. Each principle: one line + one decision it would force.

  • 10 min: cluster findings into themes
  • 15 min: draft 5 candidates
  • 10 min: test & cull to 3
  • 10 min: write the principle card
⏱ 10 cluster · 15 draft · 10 cull · 10 write · 90s readouts
