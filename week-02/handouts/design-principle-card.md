# Design Principle Card

> **Day 3 · Activity 4 handout.** Convert your audit findings into three design principles your squad can hold the line on across PRDs. Each principle gets one line plus one decision it would force.

## What a good design principle is

- A **statement**, not a feature.
- **Decision-making** — it tells you what to choose when two options compete.
- Phrased in the **product's voice**, not the framework's.
- **Memorable** — short enough that an engineer would quote it back.

## Calibrating: sharp vs. vacuous

| Sharp | Why | Vacuous | Why |
|-------|-----|---------|-----|
| "If a dispatcher can't recover from a mis-tap in 1 second, we built it wrong." | Decision-making, specific, memorable | "Be user-friendly" | Says nothing you can act on |
| "Show the network state on every screen — assume connectivity is broken." | Drives concrete choices | "Use Material Design" | A toolkit, not a principle |
| "Power users get keyboard shortcuts. New users get them visible too." | Names and resolves a tension | "Be consistent" | True but not actionable |

A principle that fails the decision test ("does it tell us what to choose when two options compete?") needs a rewrite before it locks.
