## The four engagement principles

| Principle | Plain English |
|-----------|---------------|
| **Engage early** | Surprise = anger. Surface concerns before they become decisions. |
| **Engage deliberately** | One channel per stakeholder. Avoid Slack-DM-plus-email-plus-meeting redundancy. |
| **Match form to power** | High-power stakeholders get crisp written updates; high-interest stakeholders get conversation. |
| **Bidirectional asks** | Every engagement names what *they* need from *us* AND what *we* need from *them*. |

Most TPMs do (1) and (2) well by quarter 2 of the role. (3) and (4) are the differentiators.

## Engagement style by quadrant

From yesterday's Power × Interest grid, today's engagement style:

| Quadrant | Cadence | Format | Length |
|----------|---------|--------|--------|
| **High Power + High Interest** | Weekly or bi-weekly | Written brief + 30-min sync | Brief: 1 page; sync: focused agenda |
| **High Power + Low Interest** | Monthly or trigger-based | Crisp written update; rarely sync | < 250 words |
| **Low Power + High Interest** | Bi-weekly | Email digest; group thread | 5 bullet points |
| **Low Power + Low Interest** | Quarterly or on-launch | Newsletter / launch email | Short |

These are starting points. Adjust per stakeholder personality.

## The engagement plan template

For each stakeholder, capture:

```markdown
### <Name / Role>

**Quadrant:** High-Power × Low-Interest (etc.)

**Cadence:** weekly / bi-weekly / monthly / quarterly / trigger-based

**Format:** crisp memo / email / sync / standup mention / newsletter

**Length:** < N words / < N min

**What they need from us (recurring):**
- ...

**What we need from them (recurring):**
- ...

**What they need from us right now (this period):**
- ...

**What we need from them right now (this period):**
- ...

**Channel of choice:** email / Slack / sync / Confluence
**Backup channel:** ...

**Stakeholder-specific notes:**
- (preferences, sensitivities, history with similar work)
```

The "right now" rows are what change month-to-month; the "recurring" rows are stable.

## Activity 1 — Engagement Style Calibration

**Format:** Triad &bull; **35 min** &bull; Block 1

### Purpose
Practice picking the right engagement style on real-world examples, before applying it to the triad's stakeholders.

### The engagement triage pack (8 stakeholder profiles)

Each card describes a stakeholder from a hypothetical company. The triad picks: cadence, format, length.

1. **CFO at a Series C company** — cares about cost forecasts, not implementation. Asks for ROI.
2. **Lead engineer on a partner team** — owns an API you depend on. Slack-first.
3. **Customer Success Manager for an at-risk top-10 account** — needs talking points; her customer is asking weekly.
4. **VP of Operations** — has 17 reports; her calendar is brutal; she vetoes things if she's surprised.
5. **A junior PM on an adjacent team** — high interest, low power, uses your work as a blueprint.
6. **A senior security architect** — every six weeks for compliance review; expects a written brief.
7. **A staff engineer in your own squad** — daily standup; weekly 1:1; needs detail.
8. **A regulator's outside counsel** — quarterly request for compliance evidence; expects formal documentation.

### Triad protocol

1. **Place each on the Power × Interest grid** (10 min).
2. **Pick cadence + format + length** (15 min).
3. **Disagree productively** (10 min). For 1–2 cards your triad disagreed on, document why.

### Readout (60 sec per triad)

> "The trickiest one was [X] because [why]. We chose [Y] but the disagreement was [Z]."

## Activity 2 — Build Your Recurring Engagement Plan

**Format:** Triad &bull; **40 min** &bull; Block 2

### Purpose
For each stakeholder on your map, fill in the **recurring** part of the engagement plan.

### Triad protocol

1. **Sort stakeholders by quadrant** (5 min).
2. **For high-power × high-interest (typically 3–5):** detailed plan (10 min). These are your closest partners.
3. **For high-power × low-interest (typically 3–5):** crisp plan (10 min). The dangerous quadrant.
4. **For low-power × high-interest (typically 2–4):** group plan (5 min). Email digests, group threads.
5. **For low-power × low-interest (typically 2–3):** lightweight plan (5 min). Newsletter; launch comms.
6. **Sanity check** (5 min). Are you over-investing in any quadrant?

### What "good" looks like

- **Most time** goes to high-power × high-interest stakeholders
- **Crispest writing** goes to high-power × low-interest stakeholders
- **Group channels** for low-power × high-interest (cohort engagement, not 1:1)
- **No invented meetings** — the plan respects calendar reality

## Activity 3 — Build Your "Right Now" Asks

**Format:** Triad &bull; **40 min** &bull; Block 3

### Purpose
For each stakeholder, name what you need from them in the **next 2–4 weeks** and what they're likely to need from you.

### The 3 most useful "asks" categories

| Category | Examples |
|----------|----------|
| **Decisions** | Confirm scope; sign off on architecture stance; approve SLO targets |
| **Information** | Answer a specific question; share a doc; introduce someone |
| **Action** | Add a story to their team's backlog; schedule a review; make a call |

For each stakeholder, list 1–3 asks per category that you'll need over the next month. Then identify the asks **they'll have for you**.

### Triad protocol

1. **Walk each stakeholder** (20 min). For each, list 3–5 "right now" asks (mix of decisions, information, action).
2. **List reciprocal asks** (10 min). What will *they* ask of *you*? Predict the top 1–2 per stakeholder.
3. **Flag conflicts** (5 min). Are any of your asks in tension with each other? (e.g., asking the architect to defer a decision while asking the eng director to approve a launch date)
4. **Triage** (5 min). Which 5 asks across all stakeholders are the most consequential? Those go on the radar for Friday's negotiation.

### What "good" looks like

- Each stakeholder has **specific** asks — "approve the SLO" beats "review the doc"
- Reciprocal asks are predicted, not surprises
- Conflicts are surfaced rather than buried
- The "5 most consequential" list is the input to Friday's negotiation simulations

## Activity 4 — The "Engagement Anti-Patterns" Diagnostic

**Format:** Triad &bull; **45 min** + Wrap &bull; Block 4

### Purpose
Identify the engagement anti-patterns the triad is most prone to, and design counter-measures into the SEP §2.

### The 6 most common engagement anti-patterns

| Anti-pattern | Symptom | Counter-measure |
|--------------|---------|-----------------|
| **Channel sprawl** | Same message via Slack DM + email + meeting | Commit to one channel of record per stakeholder |
| **Rolling silence** | Stakeholder hasn't been touched in 6 weeks; suddenly there's a problem | Calendar each cadence as a recurring meeting / reminder |
| **Surprise reveal** | Major decision shared after the fact | Engage early; pre-share before the public moment |
| **Asymmetric asks** | TPM only asks; never offers | Build "what we offer" into each engagement |
| **Veto avoidance** | TPM avoids the high-power-low-interest stakeholder | Trigger-based engagement from watch list |
| **Email avalanche** | TPM sends 20-page deck to a 250-word audience | Match length to audience |

### Triad protocol

1. **Score the triad's natural tendencies** (10 min). For each anti-pattern, rate yourselves H / M / L on susceptibility. Be honest.
2. **Design counter-measures** (15 min). For your top 2–3 anti-patterns, what's the specific counter-measure baked into your plan?
3. **Document in §2** (15 min).
4. **AI sanity check** (5 min). Run the prompt below.

### AI prompt

```
Role: Senior PM reviewing a junior TPM's engagement plan.
Context: <SEP §1 + §2 stakeholder list and engagement plans>
Task: Identify the top 3 engagement risks:
  1. A stakeholder likely under-served by the plan
  2. A stakeholder likely over-served (TPM is wasting attention)
  3. A specific anti-pattern the plan is exposed to
Constraints:
  - Be specific
  - Suggest a concrete adjustment
Format: 3 findings, each with: Risk / Why / Adjustment.
```

### Wrap (last 15 min)

Each triad shares:

- The **engagement style** for one of their high-power × low-interest stakeholders
- The **most consequential ask** for the next month
- The **anti-pattern** they're most exposed to + the counter-measure
