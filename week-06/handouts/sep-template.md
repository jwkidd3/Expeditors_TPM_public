# SEP Template

> **Day 1 handout.** The 5-section Stakeholder Engagement Plan (SEP) skeleton your quad builds across Week 6 — the sibling to your PRD, TCD, and TMD, and the artifact that turns a sound technical plan into stakeholder agreement. Print it, pin it, fill it section by section. Each section carries a `[Day X]` tag telling you when it gets written. Friday it drives a live negotiation simulation.

---

```markdown
# Stakeholder Engagement Plan — <feature>
**Sibling to:** PRD <link>, TCD <link>, TMD <link>  |  **Authors:** <quad>  |  **Status:** Draft / Reviewed

## 1. Stakeholder map                                   [Day 1]
- Power × Interest grid: every named stakeholder placed, with a one-line "what they care about".
- RACI for the 1–2 most consequential decisions (name real decisions, not generic ones).
- Stakeholder watch list: who could flip from low to high interest, and the trigger.

## 2. Engagement plan                                   [Day 2]
- Per-stakeholder engagement: cadence, format, what they need from us, what we need from them.
- The 5 most consequential "asks" — the decisions/resources/approvals you must secure.
- Engagement anti-patterns this quad is prone to + the counter-measure designed in.

## 3. One-page trade-off brief                           [Day 3]
- Pick one TCD Section 5 trade-off; brief the stakeholder it most affects.
- Written in the stakeholder's currency — cost, risk, timeline, customer impact — not architecture jargon.
- States the recommendation, the cost of each option, and the revisit trigger.

## 4. Meeting prep + objection map                       [Day 4]
- Prep packet for the negotiation: opening frame, the ask, the walk-away.
- Objection map: top 3 anticipated objections, each with the evidence-backed response.
- AI-augmented summary of the requirement/context, with its validation status.

## 5. Negotiation outcomes log                           [Day 5]
- 3 simulated negotiation rounds; per round: the ask, stakeholder response, agreement / deferral / escalation.
- Three-round overview: what was won, what was traded, what carries forward.
- Feeds directly into Week-7 delivery planning (sprint/backlog inputs).
```

---

## How the sections build on each other

- **§1 stakeholder map** decides *who* the rest of the SEP is aimed at — the Power × Interest placement and RACI pick the stakeholders §2–§5 engage.
- **§2 engagement plan** sets the *channel and cadence*; the "5 most consequential asks" here become the raw material for §4's meeting prep and §5's negotiation rounds.
- **§3 trade-off brief** is the *content* you carry into the room — it pulls a real trade-off from **TCD §5** and re-expresses it in the stakeholder's currency.
- **§4 meeting prep** turns §2's asks + §3's brief into a *plan for the conversation*; every objection mapped here should trace to a stakeholder named in §1.
- **§5 outcomes log** is the *record and the handoff* — what each ask resolved to, feeding Week-7's Delivery Plan (DP). An ask in §2 with no outcome in §5 is an open loop.

**The bar:** an SEP a product manager could walk into a room and negotiate from. It ships alongside the PRD, TCD, and TMD; its outcomes log carries into Week 7's delivery planning.

*Each section has a dedicated component handout — `power-interest-grid`, `raci-matrix-template` (§1); `engagement-plan-template` (§2); `trade-off-brief-template` (§3); `objection-map-template`, `opening-template` (§4); `outcomes-log-template` (§5). Friday's live-simulation scoring is on the separate `negotiation-rubric` handout.*
