# 60-Second Opening Template

> **Day 4 · Activity 3 handout.** The opening you'll use to start tomorrow's negotiation. A good opening is **practiced, not improvised** — rehearse it aloud twice and time it. If it runs over 75 seconds, cut.

---

## The four essential parts

```markdown
### Opening (target: 60 seconds)

1. **Why we're meeting** (1 sentence)
   "I want to walk you through our proposed approach to <trade-off>
   and confirm that you can support it."

2. **What's at stake for them** (1 sentence in their currency)
   "This affects <currency>: <one specific thing they care about>."

3. **What you're asking for** (1 sentence — concrete decision)
   "I'm asking for <approval / specific information / specific action>."

4. **Acknowledgment of any past tension** (1 sentence; only if needed)
   "I know last quarter's <X> didn't go the way we planned; I want
   to be sure we don't repeat that here."
```

## Worked example — Compliance owner, async audit write

> "Pat, I want to walk you through how we're timing the Reconcile audit
> writes and confirm you can support it. This touches the audit-trail
> timing your SOC 2 process leans on — events stay durable, they just
> land in the store within ten seconds instead of instantly. I'm asking
> for your sign-off that a ≤10-second lag is acceptable, and a heads-up
> if any contract needs sync visibility. I know the retention change last
> quarter caught you late; I don't want a repeat, so I'm bringing this
> before we lock anything."

## The bar for "good"

- **Under 75 seconds** when read aloud (time it).
- **Invites the stakeholder in** — it's not a lecture.
- **The ask is concrete** — a decision, not "thoughts welcome."
- The tension line, if used, is **honest and brief** — one sentence, then move on.

## Rehearsal checklist

- [ ] Read aloud twice in the quad
- [ ] Timed — under 75 seconds
- [ ] The currency line names something *this* stakeholder actually cares about
- [ ] The ask is a single, concrete decision
