# Engagement Triage Pack — 8 Stakeholder Profile Cards

> **Day 2 · Activity 1 handout.** One set of eight cards per quad (print A5, or run digital). For each card, your quad places the stakeholder on the Power × Interest grid, then picks a **cadence, format, and length**. Two of these are traps — the "obvious" quadrant is wrong. Argue before you converge.

Each card has a **Public surface** (everyone reads) and a **Hidden context** (the wrinkle that changes the answer — reveal it only when your quad has committed to a first placement, or let the facilitator turn it over). Grounded in the FieldPulse world where noted.

**Public surface**
- CFO of a 300-person SaaS company
- Cares about cost forecasts and ROI, not implementation detail
- Asks "what does this cost and what do we get?"

**Hidden context**
- Reads exactly one number and one sentence. A 6-slide deck gets skimmed to the summary line.
- Will escalate hard — and publicly — if a cost projection changes and she wasn't warned first.

## Card 4 — Renée A., VP of Operations (FieldPulse)

**Public surface**
- Ops VP; owns dispatcher training rollout across ~400 customers
- 17 direct reports; her calendar is brutal
- Fears the **training load** a Reconcile change imposes on the field

**Hidden context**
- She **vetoes things she's surprised by** — not things she disagrees with. Surprise is the trigger, not the substance.
- Calibrates to high-power / *medium*-interest: a monthly written brief plus a standing trigger — "if training hours per customer exceed X, she gets a heads-up before the number is public."

## Card 5 — Omar F., Junior PM on an adjacent squad

**Public surface**
- PM on the Scheduling squad; two quarters into the role

- The trap: quads treat him as pure "keep informed" broadcast and miss that he's a two-way source. A group thread plus an occasional 1:1 beats a one-way digest.

## Card 7 — Trey W., Staff engineer in your own squad

**Public surface**
- Staff engineer on the Reconcile squad — your team
- In your **daily standup**; weekly 1:1

**Hidden context**

- The trap: quads over-formalize internal engagement. He doesn't need a 1-page brief; he needs a Slack thread and a whiteboard.

## Card 8 — outside counsel for a customer's regulator

**Public surface**
- External lawyer representing a regulated FieldPulse customer
- Submits a **quarterly request** for compliance evidence

**Hidden context**
- Everything is on the record. Casual phrasing ("we think it's fine") becomes a liability; precise, dated, sourced documentation is the only acceptable format.
- Lowest cadence, highest formality on the whole pack — quarterly, written, exact.
